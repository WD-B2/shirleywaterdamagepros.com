Appliance leak water damage in Shirley, NY often goes unseen because the hose, valve or line sits behind or under the machine. Pull the plug only if the outlet is dry, close the shutoff valve to the appliance, and if you cannot find it, close the main water valve. Call (934) 223-1875 and the 24/7 dispatch line sends a crew.

Appliance leak water damage is the wet flooring, cabinet bases and wall cavities left when a supply hose, drain line, ice maker line or seal fails. Move the appliance only if the floor is dry and you can do it safely. Keep power off to wet areas when you can reach the panel on dry ground, and keep people away from standing water.

Four appliance leaks the crew sees most

Most appliance floods start with a small part. These scenarios describe the usual patterns.

A washer supply valve that broke and ran unnoticed

The valve or hose behind the washing machine gives way while the family is at work or asleep. Water runs under the machine and across a laundry floor, then travels to rooms beside it. Cupped or lifting floors can follow, so see the hardwood floor water damage repair page.

A refrigerator ice maker line that dripped for weeks

A pinhole in the thin tubing behind the fridge leaks slowly and soaks the subfloor under cabinets. You may notice a musty smell or soft flooring before you see water.

A dishwasher that overflowed or leaked at the door

Water spreads across the kitchen floor and into the toe kick and cabinet base next to it.

A washer drain hose that slipped

Dirty wash water may run behind walls or into a floor drain. It is Category 2 water, so porous materials need more care.

When an appliance leak is an emergency

It is an emergency when water is still running, when it reaches outlets or the panel, or when it has soaked through a floor into the room below. A washing machine can pump a lot of water in a short time if a valve or hose lets go.

We take these calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and a same-day visit is the normal goal. Appliance water soaks into subfloor, cabinets and trim, and these are the materials that cost the most to replace. Tell the dispatcher which appliance leaked and what rooms are next to it.

From the call to dry floors

Appliance leaks often spread farther than they look. The work follows a set order.

  1. Stop the supply

    The crew confirms the appliance valve or the main valve is closed.

  2. Remove the water

    Wet vacuums and extraction units clear the floor. Wand tools work carpet and pad.

  3. Look behind and beneath

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera map the wet area behind the appliance and under cabinets.

  4. Dry what stays

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the subfloor and cabinets, following ANSI/IICRC S500. Drying under cabinets may require small access holes.

  5. Document the loss

    Photos, readings and a written scope support an insurance claim.

Plugs, outlets and cords

Appliances plug in at floor level, and water pools there. Do not unplug a machine while standing in water, and do not touch a wet outlet. If the cord is wet, leave it and turn off power only if the panel is reachable and dry.

Older kitchens and laundry corners

The Shirley area was developed from the 1940s with about 4,000 four-room homes on small lots near Mastic Bay. Many homes are older and compact, with laundry and kitchen appliances packed into tight spaces. Older supply lines, valves and wiring deserve a look after a leak, so ask the crew to check them. Do not assume the valve you can see is the only weak point.

Compact layouts also mean water finds a neighbor quickly, whether it is a hallway, a bedroom closet or a room below.

Check your policy before it happens

Appliance overflows are often treated as sudden and accidental, but water that backs up through a drain may be treated differently. The New York Department of Financial Services says a typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, and that coverage for water backup and sewage may come from an endorsement. Read your policy for water backup coverage before a loss, not after.

File as soon as possible after a loss and document damage before cleanup. New York regulations set timelines for insurers to acknowledge and answer claims. Ask your insurer which parts of your policy apply to a leaking appliance.

What affects the cost of appliance leak cleanup

The cost depends on how long the leak ran and where the water went. Appliance hose failures that happen suddenly are often covered, and a leak that went unnoticed for weeks often is not. Your insurer decides, so read the policy and keep the photos, readings and scope the crew writes up.
