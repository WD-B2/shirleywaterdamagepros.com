Commercial water damage restoration in Shirley, NY covers shops, offices, restaurants, warehouses and multi-unit buildings where a burst line, sprinkler release, roof leak or backup has soaked floors, ceilings and stock. A business loss cannot wait for the next business day, and the 24/7 dispatch line is open for it. Calls come from owners, tenants, landlords and property managers.

While you wait, shut off the water at the main valve or the affected zone, move stock and records above the wet floor, and keep staff and customers out of standing water. When water in a business is an emergency

It is an emergency when water is still flowing, reaching electrical equipment, or moving into a neighboring unit. It is also an emergency when it touches stock, files or servers, or when the building cannot open. A crew is sent the same day when available, including after hours and on weekends.

Even a loss that has stopped needs fast action. Carpet, ceiling tile, drywall and insulation keep holding water. Mold needs moisture, testing and remediation follow the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and any health question belongs with a physician.

Before anyone steps in

Check for water near the panel, registers, and point-of-sale or server equipment. Do not touch wet electrical gear or walk through standing water to reach a breaker. Call 911 or the utility if there is a gas smell, sparking, or a sprinkler line that cannot be isolated.

Who is calling, and what each needs

A commercial loss involves more people than a house, and each has a different priority.

Business owner or tenant

Wants to reopen, protect stock and records, and know what is covered. Needs moisture readings and a written scope for an insurer.

Landlord or owner of the building

Needs to protect the structure, the common areas, and other tenants. Needs the source stopped and a plan for repair.

Property manager

Coordinates several units, vendors and notices. Needs clear documentation of which space is wet and which is dry.

Neighboring unit

Water travels through walls, ceilings and floors. Needs the area checked even if no one saw a leak.

Staging the work around a business

Where the space allows, the crew can work in phases so part of a building stays usable. A dry sales floor may stay open while a back room is opened up, or a hallway may be sealed off while a unit is dried. This is a plan to discuss, not a promise, because the answer depends on how far the water went.

Tell the dispatcher about entrances, loading docks, and the hours the business runs. Montauk Highway and William Floyd Parkway cross Shirley, and the Mastic-Shirley LIRR station serves the hamlet, so share the cross street and where trucks can park.

How a commercial loss is worked

The steps are the same as in a home, scaled to a larger footprint.

  1. Stop and isolate

    The source is shut off, or the affected area is isolated. A plumber, sprinkler contractor or roofer handles the repair.

  2. Extract

    Extraction units remove standing water from floors and slabs. See water extraction for the equipment.

  3. Map

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras find wet drywall, ceilings, flooring and framing, including in adjoining units.

  4. Dry and remove

    Wet ceiling tile and drywall that cannot dry are removed. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the rest, and the process is explained under structural drying.

  5. Document

    Photos, moisture logs and a written scope are kept for the owner, the tenant, and an insurer. The crew follows the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews do.

Flooding, substantial damage and permits

Under Town of Brookhaven Code Chapter 33, substantial damage is a repair cost of 50 percent or more of the structure's market value before the damage. In a special flood hazard area, development needs a permit that the Chief Building Inspector administers. After a flood, ask the Town of Brookhaven Building Division before rebuilding, at Town Hall, 1 Independence Hill, Farmingville.

The crew cannot tell you which repairs need permits. For rising water in a building, see flood damage restoration.

What shapes the cost

Nothing starts until the crew has assessed the damage. The crew maps what is wet first. Scope and price follow, are explained before work starts, and rest on the factors below. A single leak and a multi-unit loss are very different jobs.

Commercial policies differ from homeowners policies, so ask your insurer or broker what covers the building, the contents, and any time the business cannot operate. Commercial property policies vary widely, so the first call is to your broker or carrier. Sudden losses are often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood is usually excluded.
