How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Shirley, NY House

By the Shirley Water Damage Pros team · Published November 9, 2026

A leak behind a wall or under a floor gives small hints first. Here is how to read them, run a meter test, and know when to bring in a professional.

☎ (934) 223-1875

The water bill goes up, nobody has changed a habit, and no faucet is dripping. That pattern is one way a hidden leak announces itself in Shirley, NY, often long before a ceiling stains or a floor buckles. Early matters because a slow leak wets the same materials day after day.

This guide walks through the clues, a simple water meter test, and what a professional adds with moisture meters and thermal imaging. It also tells you when to stop searching and make the call.

Clues the house gives before the stain shows

Leaks inside walls and under floors rarely show up as one clean sign. Collect the small ones. Two or three clues pointing at the same room are worth more than a single dramatic stain.

  • A water bill that climbs with no change in how you live
  • The sound of running or hissing water when everything is off
  • Paint that bubbles or wallpaper that lifts for no clear reason
  • A musty smell in one room that does not clear
  • Baseboards, cabinet floors or flooring that feel soft or look swollen
  • A warm patch on a floor, or a soggy spot in the yard near the house
  • Low water pressure that appeared without explanation

Check the obvious places before you assume the worst: under sinks, around toilet bases, behind the washing machine and beside the water heater. A damp cabinet floor is easier to find than a damp wall.

The water meter test, step by step

The meter is the most honest witness in the house. This test shows whether water is moving through your supply when nothing should be using it.

  1. Stop every use

    Turn off taps, pause the dishwasher and washing machine, shut off the ice maker and any humidifier, and make sure nobody flushes.

  2. Read the meter

    Find the meter, lift the cover, and note the digits or dial. Look for a small low-flow indicator that spins or flickers when even a trickle passes.

  3. Wait with everything off

    Leave the water unused for a good while, longer is better, then read again. If the number changed or the indicator keeps moving, water is going somewhere.

  4. Rule out the toilets

    Put a few drops of food coloring in each tank without flushing, then look at the bowl later. A silent flapper leak is a classic hidden loss and an easy one to rule out.

  5. Close the main valve and read again

    If the meter stops, the loss is inside the house. If it keeps moving with the main closed, the leak is on the pipe before the valve, and a plumber needs to trace it.

Public water or a well: what your bill can and cannot show

The Suffolk County Water Authority is the public water supplier for much of Suffolk County and publishes an annual drinking water quality report. If your house is on public water, your bill is a record of what passes through the meter. Compare recent usage with the same period a year ago, and ask your water supplier how to read the figures. It is an easy leak detector that costs nothing to use.

Many homes are on a private well instead, and a well owner has no water authority bill to compare. Know which you have, because it changes what happens after a leak or a flood. The Suffolk County Department of Health Services handles private well questions, and nobody should flush a system after flooding until it is clear which kind you are on.

On a well, a pump that cycles when nothing is running is a clue worth reporting to a plumber.

What a professional adds to the search

A plumber finds and repairs the supply line. A restoration crew deals with what the water has already soaked. Those are different trades with different tools.

Restoration professionals use a moisture meter to read the water content inside drywall, wood and subfloor, and a thermal imaging camera to show temperature patterns that suggest wet areas behind a surface. Neither tool sees the pipe. They show where the water has gone, which helps a plumber decide where to open a wall and keeps the opening small.

Thermal images are a guide, not proof. The confirmation is a moisture reading at the spot.

Two situations that deserve their own page

A stain that spreads on a ceiling means water is moving from above or traveling along a line. Stop using the source and read about ceiling water damage repair instead of hunting on your own.

A warm patch on the floor, or a meter that keeps moving while the house is quiet, can point to a line below the floor. Those cases are covered under slab leak water damage, where the decisions are different from a wall leak.

When to call, and who answers

Call once you have a confirmed clue plus wet material: a meter that moves while the house is quiet, a stain that grows, or a floor that feels soft. Coverage for slow leaks often differs from coverage for sudden and accidental damage, so check your policy and document what you find with dated photos.
