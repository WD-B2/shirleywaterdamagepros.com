Flood damage restoration is the removal of floodwater, the disposal of materials it contaminated, and the drying and sanitizing of what can be saved. Flood water usually falls into Category 2 or Category 3, which means it may carry bacteria, sewage or chemicals. While you wait, lift valuables where you can do it safely, and keep children, pets and bare skin away from the water.

Where flood water comes from on the South Shore

Flooding here does not come from one source. Heavy rain can overwhelm drainage and push surface water through doors and window wells. Tidal surge and high groundwater can bring water in from below or from the bay side. Rivers and wetlands that run through the area can rise and spill over their banks.

Each path leaves its own mark. Surface runoff drags in mud and yard chemicals. Groundwater seeps up through floor joints and cracks and is slow to drain. Tidal water adds salt and silt, which corrode metal and linger in porous materials. The crew will ask where the water came from, because that shapes how the cleanup is done.

When rising water is an emergency

Call right away when water is above the floor, when it is still rising, or when it has reached outlets, appliances, the furnace or the panel. If the water has an odor, color or oily sheen, assume it is contaminated and treat it as an emergency regardless of depth.

The dispatch line runs 24/7 during storms, overnight and on holidays, and we aim for a same-day response when a crew is available. Local roads can be slow in a storm, so we never promise an arrival time. Tell the dispatcher your cross street, how deep the water is and whether a basement is involved.

Water category and what it means for your belongings

The category of the water drives the whole cleanup. Here is a plain version of what the crew weighs on site.

Water typeTypical sourceWhat it means for materials
Category 1Supply line or clean sourceMany materials can be dried and kept
Category 2Washing machine, dishwasher overflow, early-stage flood waterHard surfaces can be cleaned. Carpet padding usually goes
Category 3Flood water from outside, sewage, river or tidal waterPorous materials like drywall, insulation and carpet are usually removed

Water that starts clean can become worse with time. The longer it sits, the more the category can shift, which is another reason to call early.

What the record from past storms tells homeowners

Brookhaven took heavy damage from Superstorm Sandy in 2012, about 2,400 structures by the county's count, and the damaged properties followed the mapped flood areas closely. The effective FEMA maps in the county plan date to September 25, 2009, so check your own address on the FEMA map and the county GIS layers. Homes on the bay-side peninsula can also read the Smith Point page.

On August 13, 2014, flash flooding broke New York State's 24-hour rainfall record with 13.78 inches, according to the same plan's Brookhaven section. That is a Brookhaven-wide figure, not a Shirley gauge. It still shows how quickly drainage can be overwhelmed, so know where your sump or floor drain discharges and keep it clear.

The Wertheim National Wildlife Refuge covers about 2,550 acres on the Long Island south shore, and the Carmans River runs through it as a New York State designated scenic river. Much of western Shirley lies in the refuge area. If your home sits near wetlands or a river, check the FEMA flood map for your exact address.

How a flood cleanup unfolds, room by room

Flood work is slower and more careful than a clean water leak, because disposal and sanitation are part of it.

  1. Make the house safe

    The crew checks for electrical, structural and slip hazards and puts on protective gear before entering.

  2. Pump out the water

    Submersible pumps move the volume out to a safe discharge point. Extraction units follow on floors and carpet.

  3. Remove what cannot be saved

    Contaminated drywall, insulation, carpet and padding are cut out and bagged. Cutting the drywall above the water line is common.

  4. Clean and sanitize

    Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned and treated. HEPA air scrubbers may be used to clean the air.

  5. Dry and verify

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until moisture meters read normal, following ANSI/IICRC S500.

Floodwater safety

Treat all flood water as contaminated. Do not touch submerged outlets, cords or appliances, and do not run a household vacuum or use gas appliances that got wet. Keep children and pets away. If the water carries a sewage smell, read the sewage backup cleanup page and call right away.

After the water goes down: permits and substantial damage

Town of Brookhaven Code Chapter 33 covers flood damage prevention. It defines substantial damage as repair cost that equals or exceeds 50 percent of the structure's market value before the damage. Building or developing in a special flood hazard area needs a permit, and the Chief Building Inspector administers it.

Do not guess at what counts. Before you rebuild, ask the Town of Brookhaven Building Division, at Town Hall, 1 Independence Hill, Farmingville. Repairs that look routine may fall under different rules in a flood zone.

What drives flood cleanup cost and how coverage works

Flood cleanup cost depends on how much water entered, how long it stood and how much had to be removed. A typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, according to the New York Department of Financial Services. Coverage for water backup and sewage may come from an endorsement, and flood from a separate National Flood Insurance Program policy. Check your policy, file as soon as possible after the loss, and document damage before cleanup.

We help with documentation an insurer may ask for, such as photos, moisture readings and a written scope. State rules put time limits on how long an insurer has to acknowledge and respond to a claim. Assessment comes first. Readings and photos are taken during the inspection, and only after that are scope and price laid out for you. The end figure follows from the conditions listed above.

  • Water category and source, which sets disposal and sanitation needs
  • Depth, duration and how many rooms and floors were reached
  • Materials removed, such as drywall, insulation, flooring and cabinets
  • Equipment and days needed to dry and verify the structure
  • Access, debris and whether the home is occupied during the work
