Why the flood comes after the freeze

Ice expands inside a pipe and splits the wall of the line, often in a spot you cannot see. While the ice stays solid, it acts like a plug and nothing leaks. When the house warms, the sun comes out or the heat comes back on, the plug melts and the line starts spraying.

That timing catches people off guard. You can wake up to a warm kitchen and a soaked ceiling, or come home from work to water running down a wall. The damage looks like a burst, but the cause is cold, and the fix has to cover both.

What a crew does after the thaw

The work follows a set order. Each step protects the next one.

  1. Confirm the water is off

    The crew checks the main valve and any isolation points before starting work, so no more water enters the structure.

  2. Extract what is standing

    Pumps and extraction units remove floor water. Wand tools work carpet and padding.

  3. Trace the wet path

    Moisture meters and thermal imaging show where the water went, including cavities under cold floors.

  4. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the framing, following the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration.

  5. Hand off for the pipe repair

    A licensed plumber repairs the line. The crew supplies photos, readings and a written scope for you and your insurer.

Do not thaw it with fire

Never use a torch, open flame or space heater near a frozen pipe. A line that is cracked will start flowing the moment it warms, and a heat source near water and wiring is a fire and shock risk. Close the main valve and call.
