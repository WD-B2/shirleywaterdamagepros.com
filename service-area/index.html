What changes from town to town

Near the bay, the issue is often tide and storm surge, and the water that comes in can carry salt, silt and sewage. Inland near the rivers, the issue is more likely to be groundwater, a high water table or a basement that is below the surrounding grade. In older neighborhoods, supply lines, water heaters and appliance hoses cause most of the sudden losses, and in newer buildings the weak points are often roof flashing, window openings and drain lines.

Those patterns do not decide the restoration process, which is the same everywhere: stop the source, remove the water, dry the structure and repair what is damaged. They do decide which questions to ask first, and that is why the town pages exist.

Safety steps wherever you are in the service area

Water does not care which town you are in, so the safety guidance is the same everywhere. Shut off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking, keep people away from standing water near electrical outlets, and avoid contact with water that may be sewage or flood water. Take photos before you move anything, and call 911 or the utility for gas or electrical danger first.
