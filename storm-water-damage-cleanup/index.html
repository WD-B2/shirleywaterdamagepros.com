Emergency Storm Water Damage in Shirley, NY

Wind pushes rain through places a quiet rain never reaches. Dispatch runs 24/7 for the wet insulation, walls and floors it leaves behind.

Storm water damage repair in Shirley, NY deals with rain that a storm drives through the building envelope: around windows and doors, under siding, past flashing, and through vents or a damaged roof edge. Water damage due to rain is urgent, because wet insulation and drywall keep absorbing after the storm passes. Dispatch is open 24/7.

While you wait, set buckets under drips, move furniture and valuables away from wet walls, and keep people out of rooms with sagging ceilings. Kill power to a wet area only if the panel is reachable without stepping in water. Call (934) 223-1875 and the dispatcher will ask what you see.

When storm water is an emergency

It is an emergency when water is still coming in, a ceiling is bulging, or water is near outlets and light fixtures. It is also urgent when insulation is saturated or a window has failed and the opening is exposed to the next band of rain. Same-day crews are sent when available, nights and weekends included.

Roofing, windows and siding belong to a licensed contractor. Ask the crew whether temporary covering of an opening is part of the visit, and call a roofer for the permanent repair. The restoration side is about the water already inside.

How wind-driven rain finds its way inside

Rain that falls straight down meets shingles and siding. Rain pushed sideways by wind meets every seam, and these are the entry points the crew finds most often.

Window and door frames

Wind pushes water past worn caulk and weatherstripping. It collects in the sill and runs down inside the wall.

Siding and flashing

Water gets behind loosened siding or a torn flashing and soaks the sheathing and wall insulation.

Vents, chimneys and roof edges

Gaps around penetrations let water into the attic or along the eave, where it can reach the ceiling below.

Garage door and overhead openings

Gusts push rain under a garage door and into attached rooms or stored belongings.

What recent storms show about Shirley

The Shirley Airport station logged 2.33 inches in 24 hours on January 10, 2024, according to the National Weather Service. Even a 2 inch rain on saturated ground and a high tide can push water into low rooms, so check basement and ground floor spaces after such storms.

On August 13, 2014, flash flooding broke New York State's 24-hour rainfall record with 13.78 inches, a Brookhaven-wide figure and not a Shirley gauge. Heavy rain can overwhelm drainage quickly, so know where your sump or floor drain goes.

Storm water, flood water and a plain roof leak

Storm water damage means rain that entered through the building. Floodwater rises from outside and enters at ground level, and the flood damage restoration page covers it. A single stain under one roof spot is a roof leak, handled on the ceiling water damage repair page.

A storm can cause all three at once. Tell the dispatcher what you saw, since floodwater can carry contamination and rain through a wall usually does not.

What the crew does after a storm

The goal is to find all the water, including the water inside walls.

  1. Locate entry points

    The crew traces water from the stain to the opening it used and notes it for the roofer or contractor.

  2. Remove water

    Extraction units clear standing water. See water extraction for the equipment.

  3. Map hidden moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find wet insulation and wall cavities that look dry.

  4. Remove and dry

    Wet insulation and soft drywall come out. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure until readings reach the drying goal.

  5. Document

    Keep photos, moisture logs and the written scope together in one folder. Ask any restoration company whether it works to ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard here.

Electrical and structural cautions

A ceiling that holds water can fall without warning, so stay out from under bulges. Do not use fixtures or outlets that are wet. If a tree or line is down, or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before calling the restoration line.

Look up your address before the next storm

Sandy's 2012 landfall left about 2,400 damaged structures in the Town of Brookhaven. Because the Suffolk County plan shows those properties sat largely inside FEMA flood areas, it is worth checking your address on the FEMA map and the county GIS layers before you decide on coverage.

Water that rises from the ground is flood, and a typical homeowners policy does not cover it. Coverage for wind-driven rain differs, so read the policy before a storm.
