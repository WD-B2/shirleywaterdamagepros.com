Water damage restoration in Patchogue, NY is often a building problem before it is a house problem. Patchogue is an incorporated village of 12,408 people by the 2020 Census, about 6.7 miles from Shirley in a straight line. Only 55.2 percent of homes are owner-occupied, so many households rent, and renting usually means shared walls, ceilings and pipes. When water starts in one unit and shows up in another, call the 24/7 line at (934) 223-1875.

Patchogue also fronts Great South Bay, and the county plan describes the whole village as floodplain. This page starts with multi-unit losses, then covers the village offices, the bay-flood numbers and who calls whom.

When water is coming through the ceiling from the unit above

A leak in a multi-unit building travels. Water from a failed supply line or an overflowing tub follows pipes and framing into the unit below, then the one below that. One call for the whole building beats four separate cleanups.

Tenants should call the landlord or property manager first, photograph the damage and shut off water inside their own unit if they can. Landlords and building owners should call the 24/7 line for the building, so one crew can trace the source, check every affected unit and plan the drying across all of them.

Apartments over stores add a commercial side. A bathroom leak above a shop can soak the ceiling tiles and the stock below, and commercial water damage work covers the storefront while the apartment above is handled as a home.

Who handles what in Patchogue

A village with its own building department

Patchogue was incorporated in 1893, and the Village Building and Housing Department, not the Town of Brookhaven, handles building matters. Village Hall is at 14 Baker Street. Ask that department before you rebuild, since the county plan names its Senior Building Inspector as floodplain administrator.

Its own wastewater plant

The county hazard plan lists a Patchogue Village wastewater treatment plant and the South Street stormwater pump station among floodplain facilities. If drains back up in several units at once, tell the landlord and the dispatcher, because the cause may be a shared line.

SCWA water and PSEG electric

The Suffolk County Water Authority supplies water, with a Waterworks Road well field listed in the county plan. PSEG Long Island supplies electric power, not a village utility. Know which to call if a shutoff is at the meter or curb.

A village electrical code

The village code has an electrical chapter that regulates wiring, with inspections by the New York Board of Fire Underwriters or other authorized inspection services. After water reaches wiring, ask the Building and Housing Department what inspection applies.

While the water is still moving through the building

Do what is safe from this list, and warn your neighbors below.

Close the valve

Shut the valve under the fixture, or the main for your unit or building. In a shared building, ask the landlord or superintendent where the building main is.

Power down wet areas, only if safe

Breakers may be in a hallway or basement box. Do not touch them if you would stand in water, and tell the landlord or the dispatcher instead.

Raise valuables and furniture

Lift electronics, files and bedding off the floor. If a ceiling is dripping into a store, move stock and records onto shelves.

Keep people and pets away

Stay out of rooms with sagging ceilings and out of water from a drain, a toilet or the bay. Knock on the units below and tell them.

Bay-front flood exposure, in the county's own numbers

Patchogue's southern edge is Great South Bay, with the Patchogue River and the Swan River adding more waterfront. The county hazard plan lists the Davis Park Ferry among the village facilities in the floodplain, and names Shorefront Park and the Patchogue Theatre among its public sites. Near the water, look up your address on the FEMA flood map before picking coverage.

The plan's figures are specific. It says 500 of the village's 4,000 homes were impacted by Superstorm Sandy, that 43 properties are repetitive-loss properties, and that 353 National Flood Insurance Program policies were in force in its 2020 data. It describes all 2.2 square miles of the village as floodplain, on effective flood maps dated September 25, 2009.

A typical homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, and a ground-floor tenant should ask the landlord and their own insurer what is and is not covered. Water extraction can start while coverage questions are sorted out, so photograph the water line first.

Tenants, landlords and who owns the repair

Reference sources list about 5,776 housing units in the village, and with owner-occupancy at 55.2 percent across 5,325 households, a large share of Patchogue rents. A leak therefore usually involves a landlord, a tenant and often a neighbor, so settle early who is calling the crew.

Tenants should photograph the damage, keep receipts and ask their own insurer about renters coverage. Landlords should keep a unit-by-unit list of what is wet, since drying a whole building depends on access to every unit. Sudden and accidental leaks are often covered while slow leaks often are not, so check the policy.
