When a pipe, a washer or the roof lets go in Yaphank

Inland losses usually begin inside the house: a supply line that splits behind a vanity, a dishwasher hose that slips, a tank that seeps onto the floor. That water is clean at first, and clean water is the easiest to save, so a quick call matters. A failed hose or valve on a washer is an appliance leak job.

Roofs are the other way in. Wind-driven rain gets under shingles and flashing, soaks the insulation and shows up as a ceiling stain. Put a bucket under a drip and call early, because a sagging ceiling gets harder to repair as the drywall stays wet.

Cold adds a third cause. NOAA normals for the Islip airport station, the nearest NOAA station, show about 98 days a year with lows at or below freezing and a January average low of 24.6 F. That is enough cold to split a line in an unheated garage or crawl space, so protect exposed plumbing and learn your main shutoff before a frozen pipe forces the question.

Before the crew gets there

If water is still spreading, work through these five in order.

Find the shutoff

Close the main water valve where the line enters the house, or the fixture valve under the sink or behind the washer. On a private well, switch off the pump at its breaker only if the panel is dry.

Cut power to wet areas, only if safe

Turn off breakers for wet rooms if you can reach the panel without standing in water. A dripping ceiling fixture is a reason to cut that circuit and stay out of the room.

Lift and move

Take rugs, boxes and electronics off wet floors, and slide furniture out from under a leaking ceiling.

Keep everyone clear

Keep children and pets out of water that came from a drain, a toilet or outside.
