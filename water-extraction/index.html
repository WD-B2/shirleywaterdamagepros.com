Emergency Water Extraction in Shirley, NY

Pumps and extraction units pull standing water out of the house before it settles into the subfloor. Call the dispatch line and tell us what is on the floor.

☎ (934) 223-1875

Standing water in a home in Shirley, NY is an emergency, and water removal works best when the pumps start before the water finds the subfloor. To get a crew started, call dispatch on (934) 223-1875. While you wait, stop the source if you can, close the main water valve, and carry valuables, papers and electronics up onto counters or a second floor.

Water extraction is the mechanical removal of standing and pooled water using submersible pumps, extraction units and wet vacuums, done before any drying equipment goes in. Keep people and pets out of water that may be contaminated. Cut power to a wet area only when the panel can be reached from dry footing. If you cannot, leave it and tell the dispatcher.

What the pumps and extraction units actually do

Water that sits on a floor is only part of the problem. A few minutes of standing water lets moisture wick into baseboards, the bottom edge of drywall and the padding under carpet. Extraction takes the bulk of it out so the drying stage has less to fight.

Deep water goes out with a submersible pump and a discharge hose. Shallow water and wet carpet are worked with a wand extractor that pulls water up through the fibers and padding. Hard floors and tight corners get a commercial wet vacuum.

A good water extraction company does not stop when the floor looks dry. The crew checks with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, because water often keeps moving into places a mop never reaches.

When standing water is an emergency

Treat it as an emergency when water is deeper than the baseboard, when it is still coming in, or when it touches outlets, appliances or the electrical panel. The same goes for any water that smells, looks gray or brown, or came from outside the house. Those cases need a crew now, not tomorrow morning.

We take these calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and a same-day visit is the normal goal for standing water because wet drywall and padding get worse by the hour. Call first, then take photos of the water line and the damage before anything is moved. Photos help later if you file a claim.

If the water is still rising and you cannot stop it, say so on the call. The dispatcher will ask what you see, whether there is a basement, and where the water seems to be entering.

What happens once the crew is inside

The order matters more than the speed of any single step. This is the usual sequence for a water removal visit.

  1. Check for hazards

    The crew looks at electrical risk, slip hazards and whether the water might be contaminated before anyone starts pumping.

  2. Find and confirm the source

    If the source is still running, it gets shut off or isolated. Extraction does little good while water keeps arriving.

  3. Pump the deep water

    Submersible pumps move the volume out through hoses to a safe discharge point away from the foundation.

  4. Extract from soft materials

    Wand extractors work carpet, padding and upholstered pieces to pull out what the pumps could not reach.

  5. Map what is still wet

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show wet walls, subfloor and framing, which sets up the drying plan.

Three kinds of calls and how the crew handles each

These are typical scenarios, described the way a dispatcher hears them.

Whole-house flood after a pipe or heavy rain

Water sits an inch or two across several rooms. The crew starts with pumps at the lowest point, then moves room by room with wand extractors, and checks every wall base with a moisture meter.

Pipe failed under the kitchen sink

Water is across the kitchen floor and under the cabinets. A wet vacuum and small extraction head pull water from the toe kick, and the crew checks the cabinet base and the floor beneath it.

Water standing in a crawl space for several days

Pumps drain the space, but days of standing water mean wet insulation and framing overhead. The crawl space water damage page explains how those spaces are dried and cleaned.

Why a wet winter day matters in this part of Long Island

On January 10, 2024, National Weather Service 24-hour rain totals included 2.33 inches at the Shirley Airport station. Rain like that on saturated ground, with a high tide holding back drainage, can push water into low rooms. After a storm like that, walk through the basement and any ground-floor room that sits below the yard grade before you go to bed.

The county hazard mitigation plan counts about 2,400 Town of Brookhaven structures damaged in Superstorm Sandy in 2012, mostly in mapped flood areas. Looking up your address on the FEMA map and the county GIS layers tells you how likely a flood is before you buy or change coverage.

Montauk Highway and William Floyd Parkway cross Shirley, and traffic can slow a crew on a stormy night. Give the dispatcher the nearest cross street and note any road that floods at your end of the block, so the crew can plan the approach.

Water and electricity

Never step into standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are submerged or the panel is wet. The risk is not worth a carpet. Call the dispatch line, stay on dry ground, and let the crew confirm the power is safe before pumps go in.

Where extraction ends and drying begins

Removing the visible water is step one. The walls, framing and subfloor still hold moisture that no pump can reach, which is why a job rarely ends with extraction. Drying uses air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and it follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, so ask any company whether its crews work to it.

If the water went into carpet, the carpet and padding questions come next. If it sits in a lower level, the basement cleanup process covers sump pumps and floor drains. Once the bulk is out, structural drying and dehumidification brings the building materials back to a normal moisture level.

What shapes the cost of water removal

The price of water extraction depends on how much water there is and how much equipment and time the job needs. Insurance often covers sudden and accidental water damage, but gradual leaks and neglect often are not covered, and flood is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer. We help with the documentation an insurer may ask for, such as photos, moisture readings and a written scope.

Assessment comes first. The visit begins with an inspection, followed by a plain explanation of what the work includes and what drives its price. Price comes down to the items in this list once the crew sees the damage.

  • Depth and volume of the water, and whether it is still coming in
  • Type of water, clean, gray or contaminated, which changes the safety steps and what can be saved
  • Floor type and layers, since carpet, padding and subfloor all hold water differently
  • Access to the space, including stairs, basements and tight crawl areas
  • After-hours timing and how many rooms the water reached
